Subject: Request to use Betty's Brain for a small private learning project

Dear Professor Biswas,

I recently came across your group's work on Betty's Brain and the learning-by-teaching environments developed by the Open-Ended Learning Environments group. I found your work very inspiring, especially the emphasis on self-regulated learning and having the learner take responsibility for teaching an agent.

A little about me: I am a computer scientist working in industrial research. The reason I am writing is a small private project. My daughter (12) has been taking Python programming classes for about four years, and I would like to encourage her to work more independently on her own mini-projects rather than only on guided exercises. For her projects we use the BBC micro:bit together with Lego Technic and robotics sets for Lego from the company Elecfreaks, and we program in CircuitPython.

On the topic of AI, I have deliberately been cautious with my daughter so far. At the same time, I realize she will inevitably encounter AI sooner or later. I therefore think it would be valuable if her first deeper exposure to AI presented it as a tool for working more independently and for supporting her own learning, in a clearly technical context and within a setting that includes some adult guidance and supervision. I am building a small CircuitPython AI tutor along these lines, and your research on scaffolding metacognition and self-regulated learning is directly relevant to what I am trying to do.

Your website notes that your learning environments are available for research and classroom use on request. Would it be possible to get access to Betty's Brain (and, if applicable, related environments such as SimSelf) for this non-commercial, private use? Specifically, I would be grateful for any of the following, to whatever extent you are able to share:

• a runnable version of the Betty's Brain software that I could try out;
• any source code, if that is something you make available;
• accompanying materials such as the design of the adaptive SRL scaffolds and the mentor/agent feedback logic (including prompts, if relevant).

I am of course happy to respect any licensing or usage conditions you request.

Thank you very much in advance for your time, and best regards,
Alex Hentschel


P.S. I've attached a photo of our first project (still programmed with MakeCode Blocks): a little machine that opens a flap at the front at the press of a button, so that treats roll out for our rabbits.
